# Nil assessment periods: rules and practice

A research note for milestone 3 of benefits-modeller v2. It sets out what the law says when earnings take a Universal Credit assessment period to a nil award, what happens in the months that follow, how DWP handles it in practice, and where the model draws its lines. The design that rests on it is the [milestone 3 design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-29-nil-assessment-periods-design.md).

Written 29 October 2026. Every source below was checked on that date unless it says otherwise.

**Status.** Every reading here is the project's own. AI research agents made the readings from the cited sources, a second agent checked them at source, and the evidence on practice was checked at source when this note was written. No welfare rights adviser or social security specialist has reviewed any of it. Readings marked "for specialist review" are ones that a finding would depend on.

**What it covers.** Rules and classes of claimant: a single claimant with employed earnings reported through Real Time Information (RTI), no self-employment, no partner, no children, at 2026/27 rates. It does not describe any particular claim, and it is not advice on any claim.

## How to read this note

Confidence labels, for readings of the law:

- **Clear from text:** the provision says it directly.
- **Inferred:** follows from the text read with *Advice for Decision Making* (ADM) or with other provisions, but the text does not say it in terms.
- **Unresolved:** the text supports more than one reading, or a gap could not be closed.

Kinds of source:

- **Law:** Acts and regulations on legislation.gov.uk, latest revised versions, read through the XML data feed. None of the outstanding effects listed on 29 October 2026 touches the provisions cited here.
- **Official guidance:** DWP's [*Advice for Decision Making*](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/advice-for-decision-making-staff-guide) (gov.uk page last updated 22 October 2026; the chapter PDFs carry no dates of their own), DWP staff guides deposited in Parliament, and gov.uk guidance for claimants. Guidance is not law, and the staff guides are process documents whose current versions may differ from those deposited.
- **Official statement:** written answers and statements to Parliament, explanatory memoranda, ministerial determinations and DWP letters.
- **Official statistics:** DWP's published Universal Credit statistics.
- **Research:** universities and research institutes.
- **Advocacy:** welfare rights and advice organisations. Usually well sourced, but written to argue for change.

Sources published before 2023 are marked "(before 2023)", because practice may have moved on since.

Written answers and statements to Parliament were read through the Parliament [written questions and statements API](https://questions-statements-api.parliament.uk/index.html), and the Lords debate through the Hansard API, because the parliament.uk pages refuse automated requests. The links below go to the public pages for the same items.

## Summary

- **In law, one nil assessment period ends the award, from that period's first day.** No rule keeps an award alive at nil for the modelled class.
- **In practice, DWP keeps the account open** and checks income each month for the next 5 assessment periods. If the award would be above nil in one of them, payment restarts without a new claim. On the project's reading, this is how DWP uses a discretion in [regulation 32A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/380/regulation/32A) of the Claims and Payments Regulations 2013 to treat the claimant as claiming each month. DWP's staff guide says the account closes after 6 consecutive nil periods, after which a claimant must re-claim.
- **Surplus earnings** ([regulation 54A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/54A) of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013) carry earnings above a threshold from the nil period into the next claim. The threshold is currently the earnings at which the award reaches nil plus £2,500, a level extended one year at a time and currently ending on 31 March 2027.
- **Nil periods caused by earnings are common.** DWP counted 464,000 households in one monthly cycle in 2022. Claimants paid weekly, fortnightly or four-weekly have periods with an extra payday every year, which can take the award to nil with no change in pay.
- **Open questions** include which period's figures set the surplus threshold, whether DWP treats a failed month as a claim, whether a limited capability for work finding carries over, and what happens to a chain of claims that crosses 31 March 2027.

## 1. What the law says

### 1.1 One nil period ends the award, from its first day

- **Reading.** Entitlement needs the financial conditions, one of which is that income is such that the amount payable would not be less than a prescribed minimum. The minimum is one penny. When earnings in an assessment period bring the calculated award below 1p, the condition fails for that period and entitlement stops. The decision that ends the award is a supersession, which for Universal Credit takes effect "from the first day of the assessment period in which that change occurred". So the nil period is not paid, and the award ends from its first day.
- **Sources.**
  - Welfare Reform Act 2012, [section 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/5/section/5)(1)(b) and [section 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/5/section/7)(1) and (4). Law.
  - Universal Credit Regulations 2013, [regulation 17](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/17) (the minimum is one penny). Law.
  - Decisions and Appeals Regulations 2013, [regulation 23](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/381/regulation/23) and [Schedule 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/381/schedule/1), paragraph 20. Law.
  - ADM A2050, Example 3, in which the award "terminates from" the first day of the assessment period in which income was too high. Official guidance.
  - Regulation 32A(1)(b) ("entitlement to an award ... ceases") and regulation [28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/28)(4)(b) of the Universal Credit Regulations assume the same reading. Law.
- **Confidence.** Clear from text that entitlement fails for the period. That the award ends from the period's first day is inferred from Schedule 1, paragraph 20, and the ADM applies it. For specialist review.
- **Exceptions and edges.**
  - Regulation 28(7) treats the minimum as met while the waiting period for the LCWRA element runs. It applies only where the condition fails "by virtue of this regulation", and is outside the modelled class. Clear from text.
  - The award is rounded under [regulation 6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/6). Whether a calculated half penny meets the 1p minimum depends on the stage at which rounding applies, an open question in the [mismatch log](mismatches.md), entry 1. Unresolved.
  - Transitional protection under managed migration was not researched.

### 1.2 Treated claims in the months after the award ends

- **Reading.** Regulation 32A applies where a claim is refused, or entitlement to an award ceases, because the income condition is not met. The Secretary of State "may, subject to any conditions the Secretary of State considers appropriate", treat the claimant as making a claim "on the first day of each subsequent month, up to a maximum of 5, that would have been an assessment period" had the award continued. Treated claims therefore fall on the old assessment period dates. Paragraphs (3) and (4), in force from 22 September 2026, extend the 5 months for a claimant on a funded employment scheme. That is outside the modelled class.
- **Sources.** Claims and Payments Regulations 2013, [regulation 32A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/380/regulation/32A), inserted from 21 May 2020 by S.I. 2020/522; paragraphs (3) and (4) inserted by S.I. 2026/946. Law. ADM A2050 and ADM memo 10/20. Official guidance.
- **Confidence.** Clear from text.
- **The 5 months count from the original nil period.** A treated claim that also leads to no award does not start a fresh run of 5. ADM A2050, Example 2, requires a new claim outside "the period (5 subsequent APs)", and Example 3 says the same. Inferred. The regulation's words could also be read to apply again to a treated claim that leads to no award, which would chain; the ADM does not read it that way. For specialist review.

### 1.3 Whether a month in which the treated claim fails still counts as a claim

- **Reading.** ADM A2050, Example 3, treats a claim as made only in the month in which entitlement arises again. Example 4 treats a claim as made in each month, two of which fail because of surplus earnings. The regulation leaves the choice to the Secretary of State. It matters because surplus earnings (section 1.5) count claims, not months.
- **Sources.** Regulation 32A(2) (law); ADM A2050, Examples 3 and 4 (official guidance).
- **Confidence.** Inferred: this is part of the discretion. For specialist review.

### 1.4 What the claimant must do, and a fresh claim

- **Under regulation 32A.** The regulation requires nothing of the claimant. It lets the Secretary of State treat a claim as made, on conditions, and the ADM gives as an example condition that "there is sufficient information" to make the award. Clear from text that the regulation requires no act; any conditions are for the Secretary of State.
- **The claimant commitment.** Accepting a claimant commitment is a basic condition (Welfare Reform Act 2012, [section 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/5/section/4)(1)(e)). Whether a treated claim needs a fresh acceptance is not addressed in the texts read. Unresolved.
- **A fresh claim within 6 months.** Under [regulation 21(3C)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/21) of the Universal Credit Regulations, a new award keeps the old assessment period dates where the old award's last day fell within the 6 months before the date of claim and the claimant met the basic conditions throughout (disregarding the claimant commitment) and was not excluded under regulation 19. A claim "must be made before the end of the assessment period in respect of which it is made" ([regulation 26(5)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/380/regulation/26) of the Claims and Payments Regulations; ADM A2047), so the claim covers the whole period. [Regulation 22A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/22A) apportions the first period instead for a claimant who has stopped paid work, which does not apply to a claimant who stays in work. Law. Clear from text.
- **A fresh claim later than that.** The first assessment period starts on the first date of entitlement (regulation 21(1)), normally the date of claim. Clear from text.
- **Timing.** The 6 months in regulation 21(3C) run from the old award's last day, the day before the nil period, so the windows of regulations 32A and 21(3C) close at about the same point. Inferred.

### 1.5 Surplus earnings

- **When it applies.** Regulation 54A applies to a claim where the claimant had an "old award" that terminated within the 6 months ending on the first day the claim is for, has not been entitled since, and had total earned income "in the month that would have been the final assessment period for the old award, had it not terminated" above "the relevant threshold". Law. Clear from text. ADM H3302 and H3306 read that month as the nil period. Inferred, and supported by the ADM.
- **How it is worked out.** For the first claim since the termination, the surplus is the nil period's excess over the relevant threshold (the "original surplus"). For the second claim, it is the amount, if any, by which the original surplus plus the earnings in the first assessment period of the first claim exceeded the relevant threshold (the "adjusted surplus"). The third, fourth and fifth claims repeat this from the previous adjusted surplus. No surplus is defined for a sixth claim. Surplus earnings are treated as earned income both for deciding entitlement and for calculating the award. Law (regulation 54A(2) and (3)). Clear from text. The ADM's examples (H3311, and H3 Appendix 2, Examples 1 to 3) follow the same steps.
- **Where it goes.** The surplus is added to the first assessment period of the claim in question. If an award results, the surplus is used up there and nothing carries into later periods of the new award; the ADM says "the surplus is eroded" (H3 Appendix 2, Example 1). If no award results, only the excess over the relevant threshold carries to the next claim. Inferred from paragraph (3) and the ADM.
- **Months that are not claims.** Paragraph (3) counts claims, not months, so earnings in a month in which no claim is made do not wear the surplus down. Clear from text.
- **With regulation 32A.** A treated claim is a claim, so regulation 54A applies to it, and each treated claim is the next in the count. ADM A2050, Example 4, shows two treated claims failing because of surplus earnings and an award from the third. CPAG notes that surplus earnings "will continue to apply to the automatic reclaim" ([*Welfare Rights Bulletin* 277](https://cpag.org.uk/welfare-rights/bulletins-articles/welfare-rights-bulletin/articles/uc-seiss-payments-automatic-reclaims), August 2020, advocacy, before 2023). Inferred, and supported by the ADM.
- **Domestic violence.** No surplus is taken into account for a claimant who has, or had when the old award terminated, "recently been a victim of domestic violence" within the meaning of regulation 98 (paragraph (5)). Law. Clear from text.
- **Its purpose.** DWP's written statement of 5 March 2020 says the rule was introduced to prevent people paid large amounts on an irregular basis "from receiving a greater amount of benefit and earnings" than people with the same annual salary paid in 12 regular payments ([HCWS147](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-statements/detail/2020-03-05/HCWS147), official statement, before 2023).

### 1.6 The thresholds

- **The nil UC threshold** is (M − U) / 55 × 100 + WA, where M is the maximum amount, U is unearned income and WA is the work allowance (regulation 54A(6)). The work allowance is added unscaled, as ADM H3308 and H3 Appendix 2, Example 1 show. With no unearned income it is M × 20/11 + WA. Law. Clear from text.
- **Rounding.** The formula does not say how to round. Regulation 6 rounds calculated amounts to the nearest penny, and the ADM's worked figures do so. Inferred.
- **It is not the exact entitlement boundary.** The threshold is where the unrounded award reaches zero. Entitlement ends where the rounded award falls below 1p, about a penny lower. Inferred.
- **Which period's figures.** The regulation does not say whether M and WA are those of the nil period or of the claim being decided. They differ only if circumstances change in between (age 25, housing costs, a limited capability for work finding, uprating). The ADM's examples hold them constant. Unresolved. For specialist review.
- **The relevant threshold** is the nil UC threshold plus £300 (regulation 54A(6)). [Regulation 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/345/regulation/5) of S.I. 2015/345 substitutes £2,500 for a claim "in respect of a period that begins before the end of the temporary de minimis period". The Secretary of State's [determination](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/secretary-of-state-determination-under-regulation-5-of-the-universal-credit-surpluses-and-self-employed-losses-digital-service-amendment-regulatio--2/secretary-of-state-determination-under-regulation-5-of-the-universal-credit-surpluses-and-self-employed-losses-digital-service-amendment-regulatio), made 20 January 2026 and published 31 March 2026, says the period "will end on 31st March 2027" (official statement). ADM H3310 gives the same date (official guidance). A gov.uk search on 29 October 2026 found no later determination.
  - £2,500 applies to a claim for a period beginning before 31 March 2027. Clear from text.
  - That a period beginning on 31 March 2027 itself is covered is inferred, as [model gaps](model-gaps.md) records.
  - ADM H3309's wording, "claims made in the temporary de minimis period", is looser than the regulation's. The regulation is followed here.
- **Scale for the modelled household.** At the 2026/27 rates in the project's rules, the nil UC threshold for the modelled single claimant is at most £2,661.36 (aged 25 or over, with a limited capability for work finding and the largest housing costs element the model allows), so the relevant threshold at £2,500 is at most £5,161.36. Computed by the project from the rules; the arithmetic was re-checked by the checking agent.

### 1.7 Earnings by assessment period, and reallocation

- **Which period earnings fall in.** Earned income is "based on the actual amounts received" in the period ([regulation 54](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/54)(1)). For an employer reporting through RTI, employed earnings for a period are based on the information received by the Secretary of State from HMRC in that period, and a period with no report counts as nil ([regulation 61](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/61)(2)). So the period depends on when the report reaches DWP, not on the pay period the wages cover. Pay made late and reported when made falls in the later period (ADM H3191, Example 1). Law, with official guidance. Clear from text.
- **Exceptions.** Regulation 61(3) and (4) let the Secretary of State set RTI aside where an employer is unlikely to report accurately or on time, a reported amount is materially wrong, or a report was not made. Regulation 61(5) lets a payment "reported late, or otherwise reported in the wrong assessment period" be treated as earnings of the period in which it was received. It corrects reporting; it does not move pay that was actually made late. Clear from text.
- **Two monthly payments in one period.** For a person "paid on a regular monthly basis", where more than one payment from the employment is reported in the same period, regulation 61(6) lets the Secretary of State, "for the purposes of maintaining a regular pattern", treat one as earnings of a different period. Regulation 61(7) allows consequential adjustments. These were substituted from 16 November 2020 by S.I. 2020/1138, after *SSWP v Johnson* [2020] EWCA Civ 778 ([explanatory memorandum](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/1138/pdfs/uksiem_20201138_en.pdf), paragraphs 7.1 to 7.4, official statement; [ADM memo 27/20](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5fb25f1be90e0709ed1259e4/adm27-20.pdf), official guidance). Law. Clear from text that it is a discretion and that it covers monthly pay only.
- **Whether it applies to a given pattern.** Moving a whole monthly payment so that two are reported in one period is what regulation 61(6) can undo. A bonus or overtime paid with the usual salary as one payment leaves one payment per period, so regulation 61(6) does not apply on its words. Whether a bonus reported separately is "more than one payment" is unresolved. Weekly, fortnightly and four-weekly pay is not covered. Inferred. For specialist review.

### 1.8 What carries over to a new award

- **Nothing is carried as a stock.** Each period's award is worked out afresh from the maximum amount and the work allowance ([regulation 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/22)). A work allowance not used in one period does not carry to the next; the explanatory memorandum to S.I. 2020/1138 (paragraph 7.2) describes claimants "not benefiting from a work allowance" in such a period. Clear from text.
- **A limited capability for work (LCW) finding.** A claimant has LCW if "it has been determined" that they have it ([regulation 39](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/39)(1)(a)). The regulation sets no time limit tied to the award. DWP's "Re-claims" staff guide says a determination "remains valid" if the claimant becomes entitled again within 6 months, on two conditions (section 3.4). Neither regulation 39 nor ADM F5 says this. Inferred, resting on DWP guidance. For specialist review.
- **At high earnings.** Under [regulation 41(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/41), a new Work Capability Assessment cannot normally be carried out where monthly earnings are at or above a set level, except to review an earlier finding. [Model gaps](model-gaps.md) covers this.
- **The LCWRA element (outside the modelled class).** Regulation 28(3) and (4)(b) do not make the claimant serve the waiting period again where a previous award with the element ended within 6 months because the income condition was not met. This shows the regulations provide for awards that end on earnings and restart. Clear from text.

### 1.9 The open account

- **Reading.** DWP keeps the account open through nil periods (section 3.1). The project reads this as how DWP administers regulation 32A: the award has ended in law, and each later month is a claim treated as made. It is not an award that continues at nil.
- **Confidence.** Inferred. It rests on DWP process documents. For specialist review.

## 2. What the check corrected

A second agent checked the readings at source on 29 October 2026. It confirmed the readings in section 1 and corrected or added the following. Where they differ, the check is followed in this note and in the design.

- **The length of the window.** The readings recommended a model window of 3 periods (the period pay moves from, the nil period, and the first treated claim). The check found that if the split of earnings across 3 periods is free, the nil period can fall in the last period. The end of the award and any surplus then fall outside the window, and the gain is overstated. With the project's rates it found a household for which the window showed a gain of £2,356.55 whose true gain, counting the periods after the window, was £2,289.05. It also found that over a three-period total, a surplus can arise for many more households than a shift between two periods suggests. The fix adopted in the design is that the window's last period must have an award of at least 1p (section 5).
- **Failed months as claims.** The readings treated the use of regulation 32A as one assumption. The check found that ADM A2050's Examples 3 and 4 differ on whether a failed month is a claim, so it is a separate discretion (section 1.3).
- **A second nil period.** A nil period after a new award starts a fresh run of treated claims (regulation 32A(1)(b)) and a fresh surplus chain with a new old award. A model must allow the award to end more than once.
- **Wording.** Regulation 54A(5) says "domestic violence", not "domestic abuse".
- **After the account closes.** DWP's "Re-claims" guide says that on a re-claim the claim "will not automatically go back into payment if their earnings decrease", and that the claimant must "take part in a First Commitment meeting" and "agree a Claimant Commitment" (section 3.2).
- **Smaller points.** Regulation 21(3C)(b)(ii) also requires that the claimant was not excluded by regulation 19 (prisoners). Schedule 1, paragraph 22, of the Decisions and Appeals Regulations (the effective date for a fall in earnings reported through RTI) is subject to the claimant providing information as required. The entitlement boundary is about 1p below the nil UC threshold. And a surplus cannot carry past a successful claim even on the formula's own terms, because any award needs earnings below the relevant threshold.
- **The loss direction.** Within the modelled rules, the largest loss that the check's grid of splits found was £234.85, the lower work allowance lost in a period with no pay, with no nil award involved. A grid is a sample, not a proof; the solver query in the design measures this properly. Larger losses come from the discretions instead: no treated claim, a failed month not counted, or the LCW finding not carried over. No window bounds those. The design makes them judgement points (section 5).

## 3. What practice shows

### 3.1 The automatic reclaim

- **The latest DWP description found.** The [explanatory memorandum](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/946/pdfs/uksiem_20260946_en_001.pdf) to S.I. 2026/946 (DWP, September 2026, official statement) says that where an award is nil because of earnings, the claimant may be treated as making a new claim for the following 5 assessment periods, "with an automatic check of income being completed". If income falls far enough, "their Universal Credit will automatically go back into payment", and after that the person "would need to submit a new Universal Credit claim". It calls this the "automatic reclaim period". It estimates that about 80% of Jobs Guarantee participants working the expected hours will see their award reduced to zero while on the scheme, which is why the period was extended for funded schemes.
- **What claimants are told.** gov.uk says that if wages fall enough "within 5 months", the payment "will be automatically restarted", and that after 5 months the claimant must apply again ([Universal Credit and earnings](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/universal-credit-and-earnings), last updated 1 April 2026, official guidance). The sentence sits under the heading on surplus earnings.
- **Closure after 6 periods.** DWP's staff guide says claims with a nil award due to earnings "will not be closed immediately" and that the service "will close these claims automatically after 6 consecutive assessment periods with a nil award" ("Claim closure: Guidance" V35.0, [DEP2025-0769](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2025-0769/032_Claim_closure_Guidance_V35.pdf), deposited with a [covering letter](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2025-0769/251124_UC_Guidance_Letter_to_Deposited_Papers_Clerk.pdf) dated 24 November 2025, official guidance). Earlier versions V28.0 ([DEP2024-0442](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2024-0442/031.Claim_closure_V28.0.pdf)) and V32.0 ([DEP2024-0673](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2024-0673/032_Claim_closure_V32.0.pdf)) say the same. A DWP training brief, "UC24GEN Claim Closure" v36.0 (July 2021, released under FOI and [published by CPAG](https://cpag.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-04/UC24GEN_Claim_Closure_v36.0.pdf), official guidance, before 2023), says closure after 6 consecutive nil periods "is automated", with exclusions including "outstanding verification", "some reconsiderations" and "ongoing work capability assessments without decision".
- **How it started.** DWP's letter to the Social Security Advisory Committee (SSAC) of 26 May 2020 describes an easement under which "we will not automatically be closing claims where earnings exceed the claimant's entitlement" ([letter](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/887822/dwp-response-to-ssac-universal-credit-surplus-earnings.pdf), official statement, before 2023). Before then, an award that ended was closed and the claimant had to re-claim to return to payment; the Bath study (section 3.6) records claimants' accounts of this. In 2018 DWP told SSAC that for claimants whose only change is the level of earnings, "the average time to complete a re-claim could be just eight minutes" ([SSAC report on S.I. 2018/65](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a82287bed915d74e34020b6/uc-regs-si-2018-65-surplus-earnings-ssac-report.pdf), January 2018, official statement, before 2023).
- **Still described as temporary.** DWP's statistics methodology (updated 18 August 2026) describes keeping £0 award claims open "for up to 6 assessment periods" as a temporary policy change ([background information and methodology](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/universal-credit-statistics-background-information-and-methodology/universal-credit-statistics-background-information-and-methodology), official statistics). Regulation 32A itself has no end date, and was extended in September 2026. No announcement that the practice will end was found.

### 3.2 Where DWP's documents disagree

DWP's documents do not all describe the same process:

- The current closure guide (V35.0) also says "Most claims in which earnings or other income exceed Universal Credit entitlement will close automatically", next to the 6-period rule.
- V28.0 (2024) adds that "certain exceptions apply (for example, telephone claims or when a claimant receives a 'nil' award due to earnings or other income during their first assessment period)". V32.0 and V35.0 do not have that sentence. Its effect is unclear.
- V19.0 ([DEP2022-0860](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2022-0860/028._Claim_closure_V19.0.pdf), before 2023) says "In most cases, the Service will automatically close these claims".
- The surplus earnings guide says "If the earnings increase to a level that reduces the Universal Credit to nil, the claim is closed" ("Surplus, fluctuating and irregular earnings plus payment cycles" V10.0, [DEP2025-0769](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2025-0769/173._Surpls_fluctuate_irreg_earngs_plus_paymnt_cycles-Guidance_V10.0.pdf); the 2024 version in [DEP2024-0673](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2024-0673/170_Surplus_fluctuating_irregular_earnings.pdf) has the same text).
- The "Re-claims" guide V11.0 ([DEP2024-0442](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2024-0442/138._Reclaims_V11.0.pdf)) describes a re-claim within six assessment periods of the claim closing, "from the beginning of the first assessment period where the award was reduced to nil". On a re-claim the claim "will not automatically go back into payment if their earnings decrease", and the claimant keeps the old assessment period and payment dates but must take part in a First Commitment meeting and agree a Claimant Commitment.

The project's reading is that the closure and re-claims guides describe the account after it closes, or predate the 6-period practice, and that the 2025 closure guide and the 2026 memorandum describe current practice. Inferred.

One research observation does not fit the 6-period practice. The University of Bath study (section 3.6) reports, in a footnote, two households who stopped receiving Universal Credit after a rise in earnings and "after four months of nil Universal Credit payments their claims closed", during the fieldwork. The report does not say why. It is noted here and not weighed further.

### 3.3 What the claimant must do inside the 5 months

- **Reporting earnings.** Nothing, for earnings reported through RTI: the memorandum describes the income check as automatic, and says claimants "do not need to make a new claim".
- **A new claimant commitment.** No official source was found saying that one is needed to restart payment inside the 5 months.
- **Ordinary closure triggers still apply while the account is open.** The current closure guide says that whenever commitments are updated "these must be accepted and if the claimant does not do so there is no entitlement". A claimant is reminded to accept an auto-generated commitment within 7 days of the prompt, and if they do not, and do not have complex needs, "the claim must be closed on the following day". The surplus earnings guide says that once earnings are taken into account, the labour market regime that applies "is automatically calculated". V19.0 records that claimants on the Kickstart scheme received a new claimant commitment "when they change labour market regime due to their earnings". Whether a change of regime caused by high earnings prompts a new commitment inside the 5 months, and how often, was not found. Inferred possibility only.

### 3.4 The LCW finding on a new award

- **Within 6 months.** DWP's "Re-claims" guide V11.0 says that where a claim closes because income exceeds entitlement, an LCW or LCWRA determination "remains valid" if the claimant "becomes entitled again to Universal Credit within 6 months of the previous claim ending", provided "there has been no change in the claimant's health condition or disability" and "the date set for a review WCA has not passed". Any LCW or LCWRA amount in payment "can be paid straight away". V8.0 ([DEP2022-0452](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2022-0452/120-Reclaims_V8.0.pdf), before 2023) has the same wording. Official guidance.
- **After 6 months.** V11.0 adds that the earlier determination "does not remain valid" and the claimant "will undergo a new WCA" unless an exception applies. Official guidance.
- **On the automatic route.** On the regulation 32A route the account is not closed, so the claimant is not re-claiming in the guide's sense. No source deals with the LCW finding on that route. The project's inference is that it carries at least as readily.
- **The LCWRA waiting period** is not served again where the award ended "when their income or earnings exceed their entitlement and they claim again within 6 months" ("Relevant periods for LCWRA" V6.0, [DEP2024-0442](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2024-0442/143._Relevant_periods_for_LCWRA_V6.0.pdf), official guidance). This matches regulation 28(4)(b). Outside the modelled class.
- **No newer guide found.** The November 2025 deposit list has no separate "Re-claims" title, so the guide may have been merged or withdrawn.

### 3.6 Pay patterns that reach a nil period

- **Pay that is not monthly.** DWP's guidance sets out that four-weekly pay gives one assessment period a year with two paydays, fortnightly pay two periods with three, and weekly pay four periods with five. In those periods, the guidance's tables show the award "reduced or nil" (surplus earnings guide V10.0; gov.uk [Universal Credit and earnings](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/universal-credit-and-earnings); official guidance). The surplus earnings guide says such claimants "will always have occasions where more than one set of earnings will have been taken into account". Regulation 61(6) does not cover them (section 1.7).
- **DWP's stated position** (official statements):
  - Answer [62643](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2021-10-25/62643) (28 October 2021): four-weekly paid claimants "will still have the benefit of the higher income from their earnings", and the assessment period and payment structure "are fundamental parts of its design". Before 2023.
  - Answers [85330](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2021-12-01/85330) (6 December 2021) and [109556](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2022-12-13/109556) (19 December 2022): "no plans to change either Universal Credit assessment periods or payment structures". Before 2023.
  - Answer [2032](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2023-11-14/2032) (22 November 2023): "no plans to change the way universal credit treats earnings received in a claimant's assessment period"; for weekly, fortnightly or four-weekly pay this "may reduce, or in some cases, end the Universal Credit award"; and two sets of monthly earnings in one period affects "a small minority of claimants in very specific circumstances".
  - Answer 66811 (17 July 2025): four-weekly paid claimants have 13 payments a year, so in one assessment period they receive two, and in that period "entitlement will be reduced or end"; and "UC's monthly assessment period doesn't suit household circumstances in all cases", which DWP was exploring with expert stakeholders as part of its review of Universal Credit.
  - Answer [92427](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2025-11-18/92427) (24 November 2025): DWP "will provide updates on the review in due course". No later update was found.
- **Arrears and lump sums.** Employees receiving arrears or lump sums in one month "could have their Universal Credit award reduced to zero in the same month", and DWP is "unable to distinguish whether an increase to a claimant's earnings is temporary or permanent" (answer [201729](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2023-10-13/201729), 18 October 2023, official statement).
- **Research and advocacy.**
  - The Institute for Fiscal Studies writes of four-weekly pay that "UC entitlement falls sharply, sometimes to zero". Its footnote 8 states both directions of the effect this project models: claimants whose earnings are sometimes below the work allowance and sometimes above it "end up getting less UC than someone with stable earnings", while those whose earnings are sometimes below the point at which UC reaches zero and sometimes above it "will end up getting more UC", and the surplus earnings rule "goes some way to mitigating this effect" (Tom Waters, [*Universal credit review: challenges and options for reform*](https://ifs.org.uk/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFS%20Report%20-%20Universal%20credit%20review%20challenges%20and%20options%20for%20reform_1.pdf), March 2026, research).
  - The University of Bath followed 61 claimants in 42 working households. Participants whose wages were counted twice in one period "were grateful for the small concession whereby the claim is no longer automatically closed", and some described earlier closures that meant a full new claim. The report does not date those closures (Griffiths and Wood, [*Coping and hoping*](https://www.bath.ac.uk/publications/coping-and-hoping-navigating-the-ups-and-downs-of-monthly-assessment-in-universal-credit/), April 2024, research).

### 3.7 Regulation 61(6) in practice

- **Timeline** (official statements, before 2023). Reallocation was first done with a manual tool, and automated identification was "expected to be implemented in early 2021" (answer [151764](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2021-02-09/151764), 16 February 2021). Automated identification "will only be for affected claimants receiving monthly earnings" (answer [155189](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2021-02-19/155189), 1 March 2021).
- **Partly automated.** CPAG reports that from August 2021 the system identifies a possible double monthly payment and "creates a to-do which requires action by the case manager". It cites DWP records that full automation was not pursued because "in 25 per cent of cases this was not straightforward", and says its Early Warning System "continues to receive occasional cases" in which double payments were not reallocated (CPAG, [*You reap what you code*](https://cpag.org.uk/sites/default/files/2023-08/You%20reap%20what%20you%20code%20chapter%202-%20decision%20making.pdf), chapter 2, August 2023, advocacy).
- **DWP's current account** (official statements):
  - Answer [86273](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2025-10-29/86273) (7 November 2025): claimants with two sets of monthly earnings in one period "are usually identified by an automated system that corrects the Universal Credit award", while "a small number" are identified by hand.
  - Answer [126285](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-04-10/126285) (20 April 2026): "In most cases" they are identified automatically; cases missed include "irregular pay patterns or variations in how employers report earnings".
  - Answer [123679](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-03-25/123679) (13 April 2026): the rule "only applies where earnings are paid calendar monthly", affects "the relatively small number of households", and "prevents claimants from losing their Work Allowance" in such months.
  - gov.uk tells monthly-paid claimants whose payday changes that the award "will usually be adjusted automatically".
- **How many.** No count was found. In 2020 the government said it was aware of "around 1,000 claimants who have disputed their earnings" in the group affected by *Johnson*, and that a figure of 85,000 put to it "is not one that resonates with us" ([Hansard, House of Lords, 29 June 2020](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Lords/2020-06-29/debates/C455902E-5C19-4E32-934E-7F18A34D53B6/UniversalCreditCourtOfAppealJudgment), official statement, before 2023).

### 3.8 What the award alone does not show

- **Passported help.** Citizens Advice reports that where passported support depends on earning below a threshold, "this income threshold is tied to the earnings assessed in a claimant's AP", giving help with NHS costs and Healthy Start as examples, and that monthly assessment also affects Council Tax Support ([*A better fit*](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/policy/publications/a-better-fit-how-universal-credit-can-improve-income-stability-for-employees/), 7 August 2025, advocacy). The Bath study says a loss of entitlement can reduce or remove council tax reduction and other passported help (research).
- **Cost of Living Payments,** while they existed, were not paid to a household with a nil award in the qualifying period (answers 74834 and 201729). In the Bath study, six participants did not receive the November 2022 payment for that reason (research). The payments were made between July 2022 and February 2024 and are "no longer an active policy" (Citizens Advice, advocacy).
- **The work allowance.** A nil period uses one period's work allowance instead of two. Bath puts the loss at £208 or £347 a month (55% of the lower or higher work allowance). Bath does not say which year's rates; the figures match 55% of the 2023/24 allowances it gives (£379 and £631), which is the project's inference. At 2026/27 rates the same arithmetic gives £234.85 and £390.50, as [model gaps](model-gaps.md) records for the LCW finding.
- **Budgeting.** Bath found that "only a little over a quarter of households had any savings they could draw on when monthly earnings dipped" (research).
- **Advice demand.** In 2024/25 Citizens Advice helped "over 2,100 people specifically with issues relating to fluctuating hours" and over 2,100 with non-monthly wages (advocacy).

### 3.9 Policy direction as at 29 October 2026

- **The £2,500 level.** It was meant to return to £300 in April 2019 (SSAC report, 2018). DWP put a one-year delay at £100m (written answer [186341](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2018-10-31/186341), 7 November 2018, before 2023), and the extension to March 2021 at £70m (HCWS147). Determinations since then have each extended it by a year: to 31 March 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 ([determinations page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/welfare-reform-act-2012-ministerial-determinations/ministerial-determinations-relating-to-universal-credit)), to 31 March 2026 ([published 13 March 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/secretary-of-state-determination-under-regulation-5-of-the-universal-credit-surpluses-and-self-employed-losses-digital-service-amendment-regulatio)), and to 31 March 2027 (section 1.6). Those that state a reason say it is "to safeguard the efficient administration of universal credit". Official statements. Nothing was found about the period after 31 March 2027. Past practice suggests a further extension, but that is an inference, not a commitment.
- **Regulation 32A** was extended from 22 September 2026 for funded employment schemes. The regulations were referred to SSAC on 15 July 2026, which "confirmed that they are content for the regulations to proceed without formal reference" (memorandum, paragraph 7.1). No proposal to shorten or end the 5 months was found.
- **Assessment periods.** No change has been announced (section 3.6). Proposals outside government include opt-in averaging over recent months and a journal flag when two pay cheques fall in one period (IFS, citing Clegg and others, 2026, research), and letting claimants move their assessment period dates, wider use of twice-monthly payments, and averaging over several periods for passported help (Citizens Advice, advocacy).
- **Four-weekly pay in the courts.** In *R (Pantellerisco) v SSWP* [2021] EWCA Civ 1454 (8 October 2021), the Court of Appeal allowed DWP's appeal on the benefit cap's earnings exemption for a claimant paid four-weekly, finding that a fix would "seriously impair the workability and reliability of the assessment of entitlement". The Supreme Court refused permission to appeal by order of 4 August 2022. CPAG reports that "The human rights argument has not been decided by either the High Court or the Court of Appeal" (CPAG [test case page](https://cpag.org.uk/welfare-rights/test-cases/test-case-updates/universal-credit-benefit-cap-those-paid-4-weekly), 10 August 2022, advocacy reporting a judgment, before 2023). The judgment itself was not read.
- **Limited capability for work.** CPAG reports that the government plans to abolish the Work Capability Assessment, with an estimated date of 2028/29, after which "there will be no limited capability for work group", and that "No final decisions have been taken on future entitlement arrangements" (CPAG, [Welfare reform: health-related and disability benefits](https://cpag.org.uk/welfare-rights/whats-new/welfare-reform-health-and-disability-benefits), last reviewed 26 October 2026, advocacy quoting official statements). No decision on what would replace the LCW test for the work allowance was found. Findings that turn on the LCW work allowance may date quickly.

## 4. What remains unresolved

Each of these is for specialist review.

1. **Which period's maximum amount and work allowance set the surplus threshold** (section 1.6). The model uses the period of the claim being decided.
2. **A chain of claims that crosses 31 March 2027.** Regulation 5 of S.I. 2015/345 sets the threshold claim by claim. On a literal reading, a claim governed by £300 might measure the original surplus again against the lower threshold, and so meet regulation 54A(1)(c) for the first time. The text gives no rule for a chain. The model avoids such chains.
3. **Whether DWP treats a failed month as a claim** (section 1.3), which changes how far a surplus is worn down.
4. **Whether the 5 months chain** from a treated claim that leads to no award (section 1.2). The ADM says not.
5. **Whether the LCW finding carries over** (sections 1.8 and 3.4). It rests on DWP guidance, not on the regulation or the ADM.
6. **Whether regulation 61(6) can undo a given pattern** for a claimant paid monthly, and whether a bonus reported separately counts as a second payment (section 1.7).
7. **Whether a treated claim needs a fresh claimant commitment,** and whether a change of labour market regime caused by earnings can close the account inside the 5 months (sections 1.4 and 3.3).
8. **The open account as DWP's way of administering regulation 32A** (section 1.9), and the inconsistent wording of DWP's guides (section 3.2).
9. **The rounding stage** for an award near the 1p minimum (section 1.1; [mismatch log](mismatches.md), entry 1).

## 5. Where the model draws its lines

The [milestone 3 design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-29-nil-assessment-periods-design.md) builds on the readings above. In summary:

- **Modelled:** the end of the award at a nil period; treated claims under regulation 32A for up to 5 months from the nil period, on the old assessment period dates; surplus earnings under regulation 54A at the £2,500 level; and a second nil period after a new award.
- **The window.** Findings use 3 consecutive periods, starting in a live award with no surplus. The last period's award must be at least 1p, so that the effects of a nil period end inside the window (section 2). Splits and households excluded by this rule are reported. Where no claim is made after the award ends, the loss continues past any window, and loss figures in those cases are lower bounds.
- **Dates.** Every period begins on or before 31 March 2027, so the £2,500 level applies throughout and no chain crosses that date.
- **Judgement points,** not rules: whether a claim is made after the award ends, whether a failed month counts as a claim, and whether the LCW finding carries over. From the evidence in section 3, the design takes as its realistic case that a claim is made, that each month counts, and that the LCW finding carries over. The other cases are stress cases.
- **Not modelled:** regulation 61(6) reallocation; regulation 60 notional earnings; the domestic violence exception in regulation 54A(5); transitional protection; the claimant commitment on a reclaim; the account closing for reasons other than earnings; passported help and Council Tax Support; and late or wrong RTI reports, since the model takes reported earnings as exact.
- **Caveats on every finding.** Findings describe what the rules would pay for a pattern of reported earnings, for a class of claimant. They depend on the dated practice in section 3, including the £2,500 level and the 6-period closure, and they carry the gaps in [model gaps](model-gaps.md).
