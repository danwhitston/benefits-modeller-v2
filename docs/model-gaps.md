# Model gaps

What the model leaves out, and how each gap limits the findings. A finding is only as good as the rules behind it, so every query result should be read against this list. Entries are removed when a milestone closes them. The roadmap that may close them is under [Milestones](v2-brief.md#milestones) in the brief.

Started 29 October 2026. Legal sources were checked on that date on legislation.gov.uk.

Each entry gives the gap, its source, its effect on findings, and where it might be fixed.

## Rules that cross assessment periods

### Surplus earnings, and an award ending after a nil period

- **Gap.** The rules compute one assessment period at a time. When earnings reduce an award to nil, the award can end, and a new claim within 6 months counts earnings above "the relevant threshold" as surplus earnings ([regulation 54A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/54A) of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013). The relevant threshold is the nil UC threshold plus £300, but for a claim in respect of a period that begins before the end of the "temporary de minimis period" it is the nil UC threshold plus £2,500 ([regulation 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/345/regulation/5) of S.I. 2015/345; *Advice for Decision Making* (ADM) paragraphs H3309 and H3310). The regulation ends that period on 31 March 2019 and lets the Secretary of State extend it. The Secretary of State's [determination](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/secretary-of-state-determination-under-regulation-5-of-the-universal-credit-surpluses-and-self-employed-losses-digital-service-amendment-regulatio--2/secretary-of-state-determination-under-regulation-5-of-the-universal-credit-surpluses-and-self-employed-losses-digital-service-amendment-regulatio), made on 20 January 2026 and published on gov.uk on 31 March 2026, says the period "will end on 31st March 2027". So the £2,500 threshold applies to a claim in respect of a period beginning on or before 31 March 2027 (the end of the temporary de minimis period as currently extended), and a claim in respect of a later period gets £300 unless the period is extended again. All checked 29 October 2026. Neither the end of the award nor surplus earnings is modelled. [Regulation 32A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/380/regulation/32A) of the Universal Credit etc. (Claims and Payments) Regulations 2013 deals with an award that "ceases because that condition is not met" (the financial condition), and lets the claimant be treated as claiming again in each of up to 5 following months. The project reads this as: an assessment period with a nil award, because of earnings, ends the award. That reading is for specialist review.
- **Effect.** Moving pay between periods gains nothing beyond rounding while the award stays above nil in every period, because the award is a concave function of earnings. The milestone 2 query found the largest such gain to be 1p (see [Strategic findings](#strategic-findings-and-their-conditions)). The larger gains would come from pushing one period to nil. Regulation 54A does not block or reduce such a gain below the relevant threshold: it applies surplus earnings only for earnings above that threshold (the nil UC threshold plus £2,500 or £300, as above), and only on a new claim within 6 months. Regulation 60 (next entry) is a separate possible limit. Queries about timing are limited to splits where every period's award stays above nil because the end of the award and the reclaim are not modelled, so a gain through a nil period cannot be valued. Gains from nil periods are not reported, and the finding says nothing about them.
- **Where.** The milestone that adds rules crossing periods. Note that the nil UC threshold divides by 55% (20/11), which reaches the translator's gap for decimal rates with no terminating decimal form.

### Notional earnings limit how far pay can be deferred

- **Gap.** A timing query treats the split of earnings between periods as a free choice by the claimant or the employer. [Regulation 60](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/60) of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013 treats a person who has deprived themselves of earned income, or whose employer has arranged for them to be deprived, "for the purpose of securing entitlement to universal credit or to an increased amount" as possessing that income. The purpose is treated as existing if higher entitlement resulted and, in the Secretary of State's opinion, this was a foreseeable and intended consequence. ADM paragraphs H3201 to H3216 apply the rule, and H3216 tells decision makers to consider the timing of a disposal carefully. Checked 29 October 2026. The rule is not modelled.
- **Effect.** The claimant's control of when earnings fall is practical influence over the employer, not a legal power. Regulation 60 may treat a claimant as possessing earnings deferred for the purpose of securing or increasing universal credit. Neither the regulation nor ADM H3201 to H3216 says that deferring pay to a later assessment period is a deprivation (H3205 says income already received cannot be deprived), and whether it falls within the rule is not settled. It is the project's reading, for specialist review. A timing finding says what the rules would pay for a given split, not that the split is open to a claimant.
- **Where.** No milestone is planned. It would need a model of the claimant's purpose, which the rules do not hold. It stays a caveat on every timing finding.

## Rounding

- **Gap.** The stage at which regulation 6 rounds is an open question with three readings. The rules follow one of them.
- **Effect.** Awards can differ by a penny between readings, and findings measured in pennies (rounding gains, for example) depend on the reading.
- **Where.** See the [mismatch log](mismatches.md), entry 1, and the rounding-stage candidate in the brief.

## Household and claim

- **Single claimant only.** No couples or joint claims, so the couple finding cannot be a judgement point yet.
- **No children.** No child element, and responsibility for a child does not open the work allowance.
- **Other elements.** Only the standard allowance and the housing costs element are modelled. The LCWRA, carer and childcare elements are not. The LCW element kept under transitional provisions from before 3 April 2017 is not.
- **Unearned income and capital.** Unearned income is zero, and capital is assumed to be below the limit.
- **Earnings.** `earned_income` is earned income as regulation 55 defines it, net of income tax, National Insurance and pension contributions. Gross pay, tax and National Insurance are not modelled, so findings cannot speak about gross pay or net household income.
- **Self-employment.** No self-employed earnings and no minimum income floor, so the "gainful self-employment" finding cannot be a judgement point yet.
- **Benefit cap.** Not modelled, nor its earnings exemption. The housing costs element is bounded at 804.00 pounds a month so that no modelled household can reach the cap: the annual limit for a single claimant outside Greater London with no child is 14,753 pounds, divided by 12 (1,229.42 pounds a month), under [regulation 80A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/80A) of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013, checked 29 October 2026. With no earnings the award is the standard allowance (at most 424.90 pounds) plus the element, which stays below that figure. The limit for a single claimant resident in Greater London with no child is higher (16,967 pounds a year), so bounding against the limit outside London is conservative. Findings hold only below that bound.

## Housing

- **Gap.** The housing costs element is one input, taken as already worked out. Rent, service charges, the Local Housing Allowance cap, the under-occupancy deduction, housing cost contributions from non-dependants, converting weekly rent to monthly, and owner-occupiers are not modelled.
- **Reading.** Regulation 22(2) applies the lower work allowance when the award "does contain an amount for the housing costs element". The rules read that as an element above zero, as PolicyEngine UK does.
- **Untested case.** An element can be reduced to nil by housing cost contributions ([Schedule 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/schedule/4), paragraph 14(3); ADM F3, paragraph F3142), and an owner-occupier with earned income cannot be paid the element (ADM F4, paragraph F4020). The ADM treats the second as no amount, so the higher work allowance applies (ADM E2, paragraph E2203, Note 2). It does not say which allowance applies to an entitled claimant whose element is calculated at nil. The input of 0.00 pounds does not tell the two cases apart, and the rules apply the higher allowance. If a decision maker treated the second case as an award that "contains" an element, the lower allowance would apply. This is a question for an outside reviewer. Checked 29 October 2026.
- **Effect.** Findings hold for a given element, not for a given rent.

## Judgement points

- **Gap.** A judgement point is a Boolean or a small enumeration with no probability attached. Only limited capability for work is modelled as one.
- **Effect.** A query can say what a finding is worth, not how likely it is.
- **`has_lcw` is not independent of earnings.** The model treats `has_lcw` and `earned_income` as separate inputs. Under [regulation 41(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/41) of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013, where monthly earnings equal or exceed the amount paid for 16 hours a week at the National Minimum Wage rate in regulation 41(3), a Work Capability Assessment cannot be carried out unless the claimant receives attendance allowance, disability living allowance, personal independence payment or an equivalent Scottish benefit, or the assessment reviews an earlier finding. A claimant in that position who has not already been found to have limited capability for work is treated as not having it unless treated as having it under regulation 39(6) or 40(5) (ADM G1, paragraphs G1030 to G1034; the ADM text of G1032 still says weekly earnings, whereas regulation 41(2) now says monthly). At such earnings the finding is not a free judgement point. The model does not exclude those combinations, so the stated value of the finding may include households for which, under the regulation, no assessment could be made. Some claimants are also treated as having limited capability for work without an assessment (ADM G2, paragraph G2010), and others after an assessment that found none (paragraph G2011). Checked 29 October 2026. The effect on the stated bounds has not been tested.

## Strategic findings and their conditions

What the first strategic query found at milestone 2, with the conditions each depends on. Each rests on the rules as modelled and on the gaps above, and none has had specialist review.

- **Timing.** While every award stays above nil, moving pay between two consecutive assessment periods gains at most 1p in the worst case over the limited capability for work (LCW) finding. The largest gain in each LCW case on its own is also 1p. Z3 found it, cvc5 confirmed that no larger gain exists, and Catala's interpreter replayed the witness. The witness is a claimant under 25 with a housing costs element. The gain comes from rounding in each period, since the award is otherwise concave in earnings. Nothing is claimed about splits that push an award to nil (see [Surplus earnings](#surplus-earnings-and-an-award-ending-after-a-nil-period)).
- **Value of the LCW finding.** Where both awards stay above nil, the finding is worth up to 390.50 pounds a month with no housing costs element (55% of the 710.00 pound higher work allowance), and up to 234.85 pounds with an element above zero (55% of the 427.00 pound lower work allowance). Its smallest value is 0 pounds. Both allowances are those in [regulation 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/22) as amended by S.I. 2026/148, checked 29 October 2026. The bounds hold for the modelled household only: a single claimant, a housing costs element of at most 804.00 pounds (see the benefit cap, above), and the other gaps on this page, including that `has_lcw` is not independent of earnings.
- **Housing costs element bound.** The element is bounded at 804.00 pounds a month so that no modelled household reaches the benefit cap (regulation 80A), which is not modelled.

## Outside the model

- **No specialist review.** No welfare rights adviser or social security policy specialist has reviewed the rules or the readings of the regulations. Each reading is the project's own, recorded with its sources. Findings carry this caveat until such a review takes place.
- **DWP's own calculation software** cannot be tested from outside. The model follows the regulation text. Where practice differs, the model will not show it.
- **Decision makers' practice and guidance,** including *Advice for Decision Making*, are not modelled. Only the outcome of a finding is an input.
