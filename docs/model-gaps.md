# Model gaps

What the model leaves out, and how each gap limits the findings. A finding is only as good as the rules behind it, so every query result should be read against this list. Entries are removed when a milestone closes them. The roadmap that may close them is under [Milestones](v2-brief.md#milestones) in the brief.

Started 29 October 2026, and updated on 30 October 2026 for milestone 3. Legal sources were checked on 29 October 2026 on legislation.gov.uk.

Each entry gives the gap, its source, its effect on findings, and where it might be fixed.

## Rules that cross assessment periods

### Surplus earnings, and an award ending after a nil period: modelled within the window's limits

- **What is modelled.** Since milestone 3 (30 October 2026), the Catala scope `ClaimWindow` in `rules/uc.catala_en` chains 3 consecutive monthly assessment periods through the step scope `WindowStep`. A period whose award is below 1p ends the award from that period's first day. In each of up to 5 later months, counted from the nil period, a claim may be made on the old assessment period dates ([regulation 32A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/380/regulation/32A) of the Claims and Payments Regulations 2013; [regulation 21(3C)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/21) of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013 for a fresh claim). Surplus earnings above the relevant threshold carry from the nil period to the first period of the next claim, and on from a claim that fails ([regulation 54A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/54A)). The relevant threshold is the nil UC threshold plus £2,500, for a claim in respect of a period beginning on or before 31 March 2027 ([regulation 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/345/regulation/5) of S.I. 2015/345, and the Secretary of State's [determination](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/secretary-of-state-determination-under-regulation-5-of-the-universal-credit-surpluses-and-self-employed-losses-digital-service-amendment-regulatio--2/secretary-of-state-determination-under-regulation-5-of-the-universal-credit-surpluses-and-self-employed-losses-digital-service-amendment-regulatio), made 20 January 2026, which says the temporary de minimis period "will end on 31st March 2027"). The rules hold the £300 figure for a later period, but no finding uses it. All checked 29 October 2026. The readings, their sources and their confidence are in [`docs/nil-months.md`](nil-months.md), and each is the project's own, for specialist review.
- **Readings a finding depends on.** One nil period ends the award from its first day (inferred from Schedule 1, paragraph 20, of the Decisions and Appeals Regulations 2013, with ADM A2050, Example 3). The 5 months count from the original nil period, so a failed treated claim does not start a fresh run (inferred from ADM A2050, Examples 2 and 3). A surplus is added to the first period of each claim, carries on only from a claim that fails, and stops once an award is made (inferred from regulation 54A(3) and the ADM's examples). That a period beginning on 31 March 2027 itself takes £2,500 is inferred from the determination's wording and regulation 5. See [`docs/nil-months.md`](nil-months.md), sections 1.1 to 1.6.
- **Effect.** Findings about the timing of pay can now cover splits that take a period to nil, within the limits in the next entries. Outside those limits nothing is claimed.
- **Where.** Longer windows are a candidate for a later milestone. The [scaling probe](spike-window-scaling.md) measured solver time from 3 to 6 periods on one household and one case, which is evidence for that choice, not a finding.

### The window's limits

- **Gap.** A window of 3 periods cannot see what happens after it, and the model chooses where the window starts and ends.
- **The limits.**
  - Findings use 3 periods. `ClaimWindow4` to `ClaimWindow6` exist only for the scaling probe.
  - The window starts in a live award with no surplus carried in.
  - The last period's award must be at least 1p (the last-period rule). Without it, a split can put the nil period last, where the end of the award and any surplus fall outside the window, and the gain is overstated. With it, any surplus is used up inside the window, because a surplus stops once an award is made, and the window ends as the even split's does: in a live award, with no surplus.
  - Every figure compares splits for households whose even split also meets the last-period rule. A household whose even split ends the window at nil is outside the finding, case by case: the report marks that case's figures as not available, with the reason.
  - Where no claim is made after the award ends, the loss continues past the window until a fresh claim, so no window bounds it. In those cases the split that the loss, the spread and the largest extra payday figure subtract is exempt from the last-period rule, and those three figures are lower bounds ("at least"). The gain and the smallest extra payday figure keep the rule in every case.
  - Every period begins on or before 31 March 2027, so the £2,500 level applies throughout. The query refuses a start whose last period begins later.
- **Effect.** Findings hold for 3 periods, for splits that end the window in an award, for households whose even split does too, and for periods beginning on or before 31 March 2027. A gain or loss that needs a longer window, or a nil last period, is not measured.
- **Where.** A later milestone, with a longer window if the solver time allows.

### A chain of claims that crosses 31 March 2027

- **Gap.** Regulation 5 of S.I. 2015/345 sets the threshold claim by claim. On a literal reading, a claim in respect of a period beginning after the temporary de minimis period ends might measure the original surplus again against the lower £300 threshold, and so meet regulation 54A(1)(c) for the first time. The text gives no rule for such a chain. Unresolved ([`docs/nil-months.md`](nil-months.md), section 4).
- **Effect.** None on current findings, because every period in a finding begins on or before 31 March 2027. Any finding for later periods needs a reading of this first, and needs the threshold in force then: nothing has been announced about the level after 31 March 2027 (as at 29 October 2026).
- **Where.** When findings extend past 31 March 2027, and after specialist review.

### Which period's maximum amount and work allowance set the surplus threshold

- **Gap.** The nil UC threshold is (M − U) / 55 × 100 + WA, with M the maximum amount, U unearned income and WA the work allowance (regulation 54A(6); ADM H3308). The regulation does not say which period's M and WA apply when they change between the nil period and the claim being decided. The rules measure each surplus against the thresholds of the period whose earnings are measured: the nil period for the original surplus, and the period of the failed claim for an adjusted surplus. The other reading uses the period of the claim being decided. Unresolved; the reading is the project's, for specialist review.
- **Rounding of the threshold.** The rules compute the nil UC threshold as M divided by 0.55, rounded once to the penny, plus WA. U is 0, because unearned income is not modelled. Rounding once is inferred from regulation 6 and the ADM's worked figure (H3 Appendix 2, Example 1).
- **Effect.** In this model M and WA change within a window only when the LCW finding does not carry over to the new award (the age band and housing costs element are fixed for the window). So the two readings give the same figures in every case except those with an LCW finding that is lost, which are stress cases, and figures in those cases depend on the reading.
- **Where.** Specialist review. If the other reading is preferred, the step scope's thresholds change and the stress cases are run again.

### Regulation 61(6): two monthly payments in one period

- **Gap.** For a person "paid on a regular monthly basis", where more than one payment from the employment is reported in one assessment period, [regulation 61(6)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/61) lets the Secretary of State treat one of them as earnings of another period, "for the purposes of maintaining a regular pattern". It was substituted from 16 November 2020 by S.I. 2020/1138, after *SSWP v Johnson* [2020] EWCA Civ 778. Checked 29 October 2026. DWP has said that most such cases are identified by an automated system and a small number by hand ([`docs/nil-months.md`](nil-months.md), section 3.7). It is not modelled.
- **Effect.** For a claimant paid monthly, a finding that relies on two monthly payments in one period holds only if the discretion is not used. It does not cover weekly, fortnightly or four-weekly pay, so the extra payday figures, which describe four-weekly pay, are not affected. Whether a bonus reported as a separate payment counts as a second payment is unresolved.
- **Where.** No milestone is planned. It would need pay frequency as an input.

### Notional earnings may limit how far pay can be deferred

- **Gap.** A timing query treats the split of earnings between periods as a free choice by the claimant or the employer. [Regulation 60](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/60) of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013 treats a person who has deprived themselves of earned income, or whose employer has arranged for them to be deprived, "for the purpose of securing entitlement to universal credit or to an increased amount" as possessing that income. The purpose is treated as existing if higher entitlement resulted and, in the Secretary of State's opinion, this was a foreseeable and intended consequence. ADM paragraphs H3201 to H3216 apply the rule, and H3216 tells decision makers to consider the timing of a disposal carefully. Checked 29 October 2026. The rule is not modelled.
- **Effect.** The claimant's control of when earnings fall is practical influence over the employer, not a legal power. Regulation 60 treats a claimant as possessing earnings the claimant has deprived themselves of for the purpose of securing or increasing universal credit. Neither the regulation nor ADM H3201 to H3216 says that deferring pay to a later assessment period is a deprivation (H3205 says income already received cannot be deprived), and whether it falls within the rule is not settled. That deferring pay may count is the project's reading, for specialist review. A timing finding says what the rules would pay for a given split, not that the split is open to a claimant.
- **Where.** No milestone is planned. It would need a model of the claimant's purpose, which the rules do not hold. It stays a caveat on every timing finding.

## Rounding

- **Gap.** The stage at which regulation 6 rounds is an open question with three readings. The rules follow one of them.
- **Effect.** Awards can differ by a penny between readings, and findings measured in pennies (rounding gains, for example) depend on the reading.
- **Where.** See the [mismatch log](mismatches.md), entry 1, and the rounding-stage candidate in the brief.

## Household and claim

- **Single claimant only.** No couples or joint claims, so the couple finding cannot be a judgement point yet.
- **No children.** No child element, and responsibility for a child does not open the work allowance.
- **Other elements.** Only the standard allowance and the housing costs element are modelled. The LCWRA, carer and childcare elements are not. The LCW element kept under transitional provisions from before 3 April 2017 is not.
- **Unearned income and capital.** Unearned income is zero, and capital is assumed to be below the limit.
- **Earnings.** `earned_income` is earned income as regulation 55 defines it, net of income tax, National Insurance and pension contributions. Gross pay, tax and National Insurance are not modelled, so findings cannot speak about gross pay or net household income.
- **Self-employment.** No self-employed earnings and no minimum income floor, so the "gainful self-employment" finding cannot be a judgement point yet.
- **Benefit cap.** Not modelled, nor its earnings exemption. The housing costs element is bounded at 804.00 pounds a month so that no modelled household can reach the cap: the annual limit for a single claimant outside Greater London with no child is 14,753 pounds, divided by 12 (1,229.42 pounds a month), under [regulation 80A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/80A) of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013, checked 29 October 2026. With no earnings the award is the standard allowance (at most 424.90 pounds) plus the element, which stays below that figure. The limit for a single claimant resident in Greater London with no child is higher (16,967 pounds a year), so bounding against the limit outside London is conservative. Findings hold only below that bound.

## Housing

- **Gap.** The housing costs element is one input, taken as already worked out. Rent, service charges, the Local Housing Allowance cap, the under-occupancy deduction, housing cost contributions from non-dependants, converting weekly rent to monthly, and owner-occupiers are not modelled.
- **Reading.** Regulation 22(2) applies the lower work allowance when the award "does contain an amount for the housing costs element". The rules read that as an element above zero, as PolicyEngine UK does.
- **Untested case.** An element can be reduced to nil by housing cost contributions ([Schedule 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/schedule/4), paragraph 14(3); ADM F3, paragraph F3142), and an owner-occupier with earned income cannot be paid the element (ADM F4, paragraph F4020). The ADM treats the second as no amount, so the higher work allowance applies (ADM E2, paragraph E2203, Note 2). It does not say which allowance applies to an entitled claimant whose element is calculated at nil. The input of 0.00 pounds does not tell the two cases apart, and the rules apply the higher allowance. If a decision maker treated the second case as an award that "contains" an element, the lower allowance would apply. This is a question for an outside reviewer. Checked 29 October 2026.
- **Effect.** Findings hold for a given element, not for a given rent.

## Judgement points

- **Gap.** A judgement point is a Boolean or a small enumeration with no probability attached. Four are modelled: limited capability for work (`has_lcw`), and from milestone 3 the three below.
- **Effect.** A query can say what a finding is worth, not how likely it is. The window query gives figures for every case and marks one realistic case for each value of `has_lcw`, from the evidence on practice in [`docs/nil-months.md`](nil-months.md), section 3. The evidence sets which case is realistic; it does not say how often DWP decides otherwise.
- **`claim_after_end`: whether a claim is made after the award ends.** A treated claim under regulation 32A is the Secretary of State's discretion ("may"), and a fresh claim is the claimant's act. Realistic case: true. DWP checks income each month for the 5 assessment periods after a nil period and restarts payment without a new claim (the "automatic reclaim period" in the [explanatory memorandum](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/946/pdfs/uksiem_20260946_en_001.pdf) to S.I. 2026/946, September 2026; DWP's "Claim closure" staff guide V35.0, DEP2025-0769, which closes the account after 6 consecutive nil periods). No figures on failed reclaims were found. False is the stress case of an account closed early with no fresh claim.
- **`claim_each_month`: whether a month in which the claim fails still counts as a claim.** ADM A2050, Example 3, treats a claim only in the month in which entitlement arises again, and Example 4 in each month. It changes how far a surplus is worn down, since regulation 54A counts claims, not months. Realistic case: true, because DWP describes the claimant as treated as claiming for each of the following 5 assessment periods, with an automatic check of income each month; this is the project's reading of DWP's process documents ([`docs/nil-months.md`](nil-months.md), sections 1.3 and 3.1). It matters only when `claim_after_end` is true.
- **`lcw_carries_over`: whether the LCW finding carries over to the new award.** [Regulation 39](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/39) sets no such rule, and the ADM does not say. DWP's "Re-claims" staff guide (V11.0, DEP2024-0442) says a determination "remains valid" if entitlement returns within 6 months, the condition has not changed and the review date has not passed. No source deals with the automatic route under regulation 32A, where the account stays open; the project infers that the finding carries at least as readily there. Realistic case: true. False is the stress case of a finding lost on the new award, which then stays lost for the rest of that award within the window (the project's reading, for specialist review). It matters only when `has_lcw` is true.
- **Sources** for the three points were checked on 29 October 2026; the staff guides are process documents, not law, and later versions may differ.
- **`has_lcw` is not independent of earnings.** The model treats `has_lcw` and `earned_income` as separate inputs. Under [regulation 41(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/41) of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013, where monthly earnings equal or exceed the amount paid for 16 hours a week at the National Minimum Wage rate in regulation 41(3), a Work Capability Assessment cannot be carried out unless the claimant receives attendance allowance, disability living allowance, personal independence payment or an equivalent Scottish benefit, or the assessment reviews an earlier finding. A claimant in that position who has not already been found to have limited capability for work is treated as not having it unless treated as having it under regulation 39(6) or 40(5) (ADM G1, paragraphs G1030 to G1034; the ADM text of G1032 still says weekly earnings, whereas regulation 41(2) now says monthly). At such earnings the finding is not a free judgement point. The model does not exclude those combinations, so the stated value of the finding may include households for which, under the regulation, no assessment could be made. Some claimants are also treated as having limited capability for work without an assessment (ADM G2, paragraph G2010), and others after an assessment that found none (paragraph G2011). Checked 29 October 2026. The effect on the stated bounds has not been tested.

## Strategic findings and their conditions

What the strategic queries found, with the conditions each depends on. Each rests on the rules as modelled and on the gaps above, and none has had specialist review. The milestone 3 findings come first, then those of milestone 2.

### Milestone 3: the timing of pay over a window with nil periods

Run on 30 October 2026: `uv run bm query window build/claim_window.bm-ir.json --inputs rules/claim_window.inputs.toml --timeout-ms 600000`, at code commit `f711fce`, with Catala 1.3.0, Z3 5.3.2 and cvc5 1.4.2. The exact rules, not the relaxed translation, over 3 consecutive assessment periods starting 6 April, 6 May and 6 June 2026. Nothing was fixed: the age band, the housing costs element (0 to 804.00 pounds), the earnings in each period (0 to 10,000.00 pounds) and so the total over the window were free, and the four judgement points were expanded into 9 cases. The run took 22 minutes on one Apple Silicon Mac, with a limit of 600 seconds on each Z3 search and on each cvc5 confirmation. A second run of the same command for the text form, alongside it, gave the same figures and the same two *unknown* figures. 43 of the 45 figures are optimal: Z3 found each, cvc5 confirmed that no better value exists, and Catala's interpreter replayed every witness to the penny. Two figures are *unknown* (below): the solvers did not settle them in the time allowed, which is not a result of nil.

The two cases with a claim after the end and the LCW finding lost were run again on 30 October 2026, at commit `a56ace9`, after a fix to the rules: in the first run a finding lost on a new award came back from the new award's second period. The command was the same, with `--fix has_lcw=true --fix lcw_carries_over=false --fix claim_after_end=true` and `--fix claim_each_month=true`, then `false`. All 10 figures are optimal, confirmed by cvc5 and replayed through Catala, and each equals the first run's figure, although the solver's examples differ. The table gives the re-run's figures. No other case could change: with `lcw_carries_over` true, or with no LCW finding, the finding used is the same in every period under both versions of the rules, and with no claim after the end there is no new award.

**What the figures mean.** Each compares splits of one total of earnings over the window, for the same household:

- **gain:** how much more the award over the window can be with some split than with the even split;
- **loss:** how much less it can be;
- **spread:** the largest difference between the awards of any two splits;
- **extra payday:** for a claimant paid every four weeks, the award with four equal payments spread evenly less the award when they fall as P, 2P, P, which is what happens when two paydays fall in one assessment period. Its largest value is the most the pattern can cost; its smallest, when negative, is the most it can pay over the even spread.

**Conditions on every figure.** The window's limits [above](#the-windows-limits): 3 periods, a start in a live award with no surplus, periods beginning on or before 31 March 2027, and splits whose last period has an award of at least 1p. Every figure speaks only about households whose even split also meets that last-period rule. In the cases with no claim after the award ends, the loss, the spread and the largest extra payday figure are lower bounds ("at least"), because the loss carries on past the window.

**The realistic cases first.** A claim is made after the award ends, each failed month counts as a claim, and an LCW finding carries over (the evidence is under [Judgement points](#judgement-points)). There is one realistic case for each value of `has_lcw`.

The two rows with LCW and no claim differ only in `lcw_carries_over`, which has no effect when no claim is made; the expansion into cases keeps both. The *unknown* in the second of them is still unknown, although the first row's figure for the same rules is −0.02 pounds. What the figures say, for the modelled class of single claimant with employed earnings:

- **Gain, realistic cases: 1,375.02 pounds over 3 periods,** with or without an LCW finding. That is 2p more than 55% of 2,500 pounds (1,375.00 pounds). The project's reading of why, checked against the witnesses: in a period whose award is already nil, further earnings reduce no award, and regulation 54A carries into a later claim only earnings above the relevant threshold, which in the temporary de minimis period is 2,500 pounds above the nil UC threshold. Under an even split the same earnings would be tapered at 55%. So the 2,500 pound level bounds the gain at 55% of 2,500 pounds, plus rounding, and the bound would change with that level. The 2p comes from rounding: each period's earnings deduction is rounded to the penny (regulation 6, as the rules read it), and so is the nil UC threshold. The Institute for Fiscal Studies describes the same direction of effect: claimants whose earnings are sometimes below the point at which the award reaches nil and sometimes above it "will end up getting more UC", and the surplus earnings rule "goes some way to mitigating this effect" ([*Universal credit review*](https://ifs.org.uk/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFS%20Report%20-%20Universal%20credit%20review%20challenges%20and%20options%20for%20reform_1.pdf), March 2026, footnote 8; see [`docs/nil-months.md`](nil-months.md), section 3.6). Where an LCW finding is lost on the new award, the gain is lower (1,228.87 pounds), and where no claim is made after the end it is rounding only (2p, with or without an LCW finding).
- **Loss, realistic cases: 469.71 pounds with an LCW finding, and 2p without one.** Without an LCW finding there is no work allowance for this class, the award is linear in earnings while it stays above nil, and the loss is rounding. With one, the solver's example has no nil period at all: the loss is two periods' unused lower work allowance (2 × 234.85 pounds, the value of the lower work allowance (55% of 427.00 pounds), plus 1p of rounding). The check's grid of splits had found one period's worth, 234.85 pounds ([`docs/nil-months.md`](nil-months.md), section 2); the solver's search covers every split within the window's limits. In the stress cases the loss is larger: 781.02 pounds where the LCW finding is lost, and, where no claim is made after the award ends, at least 2,927.49 pounds with an LCW finding and at least 2,457.81 pounds without. No window bounds the last two.
- **Spread, realistic cases: 1,375.02 pounds.** Up to 1,609.87 pounds where the LCW finding is lost. Where no claim is made, at least 2,927.51 pounds with an LCW finding and at least 2,457.81 pounds without.
- **Extra payday, realistic case with LCW:** over the window, the pattern P, 2P, P pays from 731.87 pounds more to 195.25 pounds less than the even spread of the same four payments, depending on the household and the amount of each payment. Without an LCW finding it pays from 614.44 pounds more to 0.02 pounds less. So for some households in this class an extra payday in one period raises the award over the window, through the same mechanism as the gain, and for others it lowers it, through the work allowance. Where no claim is made after the award ends, it can cost some households at least 731.88 pounds with an LCW finding, or at least 614.46 pounds without (lower bounds, as above); the smallest figure in those cases is −0.02 pounds where it is known.

**What the witnesses are.** For each figure the query shows the household and splits at which the solver found the optimum. Each is one example of the class that the solver happened to return, not the only one, and not advice on any claim. Which household Z3 returns depends on its search.

**Caveats on every milestone 3 figure.**

- Passported help (such as help with NHS costs and Healthy Start) and Council Tax Support are not modelled: only the Universal Credit award is counted.
- Reported earnings are taken as exact, although RTI reports can be late or wrong; reporting and any later correction are not modelled.
- The realistic case rests on DWP's practice of checking income each month after a nil period, and the other cases model departures from it. The 2,500 pounds is added to the nil UC threshold to give the relevant threshold, and is currently set to end on 31 March 2027 (S.I. 2015/345 regulation 5, and the Secretary of State's determination of 20 January 2026). Both are as at 29 October 2026; see [`docs/nil-months.md`](nil-months.md).
- Regulation 60 on notional earnings (above) may limit how far a claimant can choose when pay falls, and regulation 61(6) may move one of two monthly payments reported in one period (above). A figure says what the rules would pay for a pattern of reported earnings, not that the pattern is open to a claimant.
- The LCW finding is treated as independent of earnings, although regulation 41(2) limits assessments at higher monthly earnings, and several witnesses with an LCW finding have high earnings in one period; see `has_lcw` under [Judgement points](#judgement-points).
- The readings of the law that the figures rest on are listed under [Surplus earnings](#surplus-earnings-and-an-award-ending-after-a-nil-period-modelled-within-the-windows-limits) and in [`docs/nil-months.md`](nil-months.md), and each is for specialist review. The figures in the cases where the LCW finding is lost also depend on which period's thresholds set a surplus (above).

### Milestone 2: two assessment periods above nil

- **Timing.** While every award stays above nil, moving pay between two consecutive assessment periods gains at most 1p in the worst case over the limited capability for work (LCW) finding. The largest gain in each LCW case on its own is also 1p. Z3 found it, cvc5 confirmed that no larger gain exists, and Catala's interpreter replayed the witness. One witness found was a claimant under 25 with a housing costs element; which household Z3 returns depends on its search, so another run may give a different one. The gain comes from rounding in each period, since the award is otherwise concave in earnings. This finding says nothing about splits that push an award to nil; the milestone 3 findings above do, within the window's limits.
- **Value of the LCW finding.** Where both awards stay above nil, the finding is worth up to 390.50 pounds a month with no housing costs element (55% of the 710.00 pound higher work allowance), and up to 234.85 pounds with an element above zero (55% of the 427.00 pound lower work allowance). Its smallest value is 0 pounds. Both allowances are those in [regulation 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/376/regulation/22) as amended by S.I. 2026/148, checked 29 October 2026. The bounds hold for the modelled household only: a single claimant, a housing costs element of at most 804.00 pounds (see the benefit cap, above), and the other gaps on this page, including that `has_lcw` is not independent of earnings.
- **Housing costs element bound.** The element is bounded at 804.00 pounds a month so that no modelled household reaches the benefit cap (regulation 80A), which is not modelled.

## Outside the model

- **No specialist review.** No welfare rights adviser or social security policy specialist has reviewed the rules or the readings of the regulations. Each reading is the project's own, recorded with its sources. Findings carry this caveat until such a review takes place.
- **DWP's own calculation software** cannot be tested from outside. The model follows the regulation text. Where practice differs, the model will not show it.
- **Decision makers' practice and guidance,** including *Advice for Decision Making*, are not modelled. Only the outcome of a finding is an input.
